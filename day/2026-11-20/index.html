Summary of the day · Friday, 20 November 2026

EU Commission confirms AI-assisted cyberattack; House reveals 879,000 China chip approvals

Two security stories led the day. The European Commission confirmed that attackers used an AI tool in a cyber campaign that took its payroll, medical reimbursement and translation systems offline for over 10 days, raising Catastrophic Risk by 0.64. House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast released licence data showing Commerce approved more than 879,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips for Chinese firms, some of them military-linked, between December and May. That added to Capability Velocity (+0.50) and lowered Power Concentration (-0.32). Smaller steps: California's attorney general subpoenaed Nvidia over possible China access to its chips, Signal switched on default blocks against AI assistants reading chats, and over 540 Google workers petitioned for AI-free Search in schools. Self-Destruction rose most, followed by Conquerors, while 1984 fell. Successors still leads the families. The Horizon Balance fell to -12.18 from -11.96.

Biggest movers. Self-Destruction +0.7, from the EU Commission cyberattack (+0.70), partly offset by fading earlier news (-0.04). Conquerors +0.3, from the China chip approvals data (+0.25) and the EU Commission cyberattack (+0.19), partly offset by fading earlier news (-0.12). 1984 -0.3, from the China chip approvals data (-0.28) and Signal's AI-assistant blocks (-0.05), partly offset by fading earlier news (+0.03).

Events scored

  1. House panel data: Commerce approved 879,000+ Nvidia/AMD AI chips for Chinese firms

    House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast released BIS licence data showing that from Dec 2025 to May 2026 BIS approved more than 879,000 Nvidia H200 and equivalent AMD AI chips for Chinese firms, out of 3.4 million requested. Recipients include Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Baidu, plus four military-backed firms or entities on the DoD Chinese military company list, according to Reuters; two of the firms were flagged as suspected smuggling fronts.

  2. European Commission confirms AI-assisted cyberattack that disrupted services for 10+ days

    A Commission spokesperson confirmed attackers used an AI tool in a campaign detected Oct 22, forcing systems including Sysper payroll and MyPMO offline for over 10 days. The attack is largely contained, with no attribution or ransom demand; researchers link it to the ARTEX framework, but the Commission did not name the tool.

    Safety & Alignment · T2 clear step · source grade B (major outlet) · structural · moves Catastrophic Risk ↑

    securityweek.com ↗scworld.com ↗

  3. California AG subpoenas Nvidia over possible China access to chips and confidential data

    California AG Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia, probing whether China obtained its advanced chips or confidential information and whether Nvidia misled investors or the public. It follows Jensen Huang's resignation over an affair with a suspected Chinese spy; Colette Kress is interim CEO.

  4. Signal turns on default blocks against AI assistants reading chats

    Signal is turning on by default settings that block screenshots and screen access, and shows pop-ups when an AI assistant tries to read the screen. It names Siri AI, Gemini Intelligence, Microsoft Recall and Meta's Muse, and urges Apple, Google and Microsoft to give developers firmer blocking tools.

  5. 540+ Google workers petition to let schools turn off AI in Search

    More than 540 Alphabet Workers Union members petitioned for schools to be able to disable AI features in Google Search without losing Search. NYC, LA and Chicago districts are reportedly weighing alternatives.
