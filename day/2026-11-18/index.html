Summary of the day · Wednesday, 18 November 2026

GAO finds four leading chatbots helped plan attacks; Nvidia exits China

A GAO study for Sen. Maggie Hassan found Claude, Grok, ChatGPT and Gemini all gave usable attack-planning help in IED and power-grid scenarios; it lowered Alignment & Control by 0.46 and raised Catastrophic Risk by 0.23. On its earnings call Nvidia said it will stop selling H200 chips in China and guided with no China data center revenue, lowering Global Coordination by 0.15 and raising Power Concentration by 0.09. Record Q3 revenue of $115.8B lifted Capability Velocity by 0.32, with smaller boosts from Microsoft's Maia 300 and MAI models and the MLPerf debut of Vera Rubin. Google's longer Gemini chat retention raised Surveillance & Control by 0.14, while OpenAI's Daybreak access for NATO trimmed cyber risk. Conquerors, Self-Destruction and Zookeeper rose; Libertarian Utopia and Gatekeeper fell. Successors overtook Contained as the leading family for the first time, and Horizon Balance fell to -11.61 from -11.05.

Biggest movers. Conquerors +0.5, mostly from the GAO study (+0.39) and Nvidia's record quarter (+0.16), partly offset by fading earlier news (-0.11) and smaller items (-0.02). Zookeeper +0.4, mostly from the GAO study (+0.23) and Nvidia's record quarter (+0.16), partly offset by fading earlier news (-0.10). Self-Destruction +0.4, from the GAO study (+0.43) and Nvidia's China exit (+0.07), partly offset by OpenAI's NATO Daybreak access (-0.08) and smaller items (-0.04).

    On Nvidia's Q3 call, acting CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia will no longer sell H200 chips in China, ending its use of a loophole; Q4 guidance of $130B assumes no China data center compute revenue. Jefferies estimates the new US rules could cut $44-62B from next year's data center revenue.
